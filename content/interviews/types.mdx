AceInt offers three interview types to match different stages of your hiring funnel and the kind of signal you need from candidates. Each type uses the same AI voice interviewer and scoring infrastructure, but differs in the mix of questions asked, the evaluation dimensions applied, and the typical session length.

Choose the right type upfront — it determines which question bank templates are available and which default rubric dimensions are pre-loaded when you configure the interview.

## Technical Screen

The Technical Screen focuses exclusively on role-specific technical questions. The AI interviewer asks a set of questions from your technical question bank, then generates follow-up questions based on each answer to probe depth of knowledge and problem-solving approach.

This type is best suited for early-funnel filtering of technical roles where you need to quickly separate candidates with genuine hands-on experience from those with surface-level familiarity.

**What the AI evaluates:** Technical Accuracy, Problem-Solving Depth, Clarity of Explanation.

## Behavioral Screen

The Behavioral Screen uses competency-based questions in the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). The AI prompts candidates to describe specific past experiences and probes for concrete details when answers are vague or incomplete.

This type works well for roles where soft skills, culture fit, or leadership competencies are the primary screening criteria — or as a complement to a separate technical assessment.

**What the AI evaluates:** Communication Clarity, Adaptability, Teamwork, Leadership, Problem-Solving.

## Full Interview

The Full Interview combines both a technical section and a behavioral section in a single session. The AI transitions between sections automatically, signaling the shift to the candidate. Scores for each section are reported separately in the results, and an overall composite score is calculated using the weights defined in your rubric.

This type is best when you want a single, comprehensive session rather than scheduling two separate screens — particularly useful for mid-funnel interviews or roles where both technical and behavioral signals are equally important.

**What the AI evaluates:** All dimensions from both Technical Screen and Behavioral Screen.

## Comparison table

| Type | Typical Duration | Question Types | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical Screen | 20 – 35 min | Technical + AI follow-ups | Early-funnel filtering of technical roles |
| Behavioral Screen | 20 – 30 min | STAR-format competency questions | Soft-skill and culture-fit screening |
| Full Interview | 40 – 60 min | Technical + Behavioral + follow-ups | Comprehensive mid-funnel assessment |

<Callout title="Note">
Durations vary based on question bank size, follow-up depth settings, and how much the candidate speaks. Set a **maximum session length** in the interview configuration to enforce a hard time limit if needed.
</Callout>

## How to choose

Use the following guidance to select the right type for your situation:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I'm screening a high volume of applicants quickly">
    Use **Technical Screen** or **Behavioral Screen** depending on the role. Shorter, focused sessions increase candidate completion rates and give you a fast signal for filtering.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I need both technical and soft-skill signal in one session">
    Use **Full Interview**. It reduces friction for candidates (one session instead of two) and gives your team a complete picture before the first human conversation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The role is non-technical but communication is critical">
    Use **Behavioral Screen**. Configure a rubric that weights Communication Clarity and Adaptability heavily.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I want to replace an existing phone screen">
    Use **Behavioral Screen** as a direct replacement for unstructured recruiter calls. It evaluates the same competencies but at scale and with consistent scoring.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
