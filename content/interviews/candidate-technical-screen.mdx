title: "Technical Screen: AI Interview Candidate Experience"
description: "Understand how the AI delivers technical questions, how follow-up logic works, how time limits apply, and how to ask for clarification during the screen."
---

The technical screen is a structured spoken interview in which the AI asks role-specific technical questions and follows up based on the candidate's answers. There is no coding environment or screen share — the evaluation is entirely based on spoken explanation, so candidates should focus on articulating their thinking clearly and in depth.

## How the AI delivers questions

The AI reads each question aloud at the configured speaking pace and waits for the candidate to respond. After the candidate finishes speaking, the AI either:

- **Asks a follow-up question** — if the answer was incomplete, vague, or touched on a topic worth exploring further.
- **Moves to the next question** — if the answer was sufficiently complete and the configured follow-up depth has been exhausted.

The transition between questions is smooth and conversational. The AI does not announce a score or give feedback during the session — it simply moves forward.

## Follow-up logic

Follow-up questions are generated dynamically based on what the candidate said. Common follow-up patterns include:

- **Clarification** — "You mentioned X — can you tell me more about how that worked?"
- **Trade-off probing** — "What were the trade-offs of that approach versus an alternative?"
- **Edge case testing** — "How would your solution handle [specific edge case]?"
- **Depth probing** — "Why did you choose that approach over [alternative]?"

The number of follow-ups per question is controlled by the **follow-up depth** setting in the question bank (Light, Standard, or Deep). Candidates should not assume the session is nearly over just because follow-ups stop — the AI may ask several primary questions with few follow-ups each, or a small number of questions with many follow-ups.

## Time limits

If the interviewer has configured a maximum session length, a visual timer is displayed in the browser during the interview. The candidate can see how much time remains.

Individual question time limits, if set, are announced by the AI at the start of each question: _"You have up to two minutes to answer this question."_

<Callout title="Tip">
Candidates should aim for answers that are thorough but focused — typically 90 seconds to 3 minutes per question. Very short answers often trigger follow-ups that extend the session; very long answers with no clear structure can hurt the Communication Clarity dimension score.
</Callout>

## Asking for clarification

Candidates can ask the AI to clarify a question at any time. To do so, the candidate simply says something like _"Could you repeat the question?"_ or _"Can you clarify what you mean by X?"_ The AI will restate or elaborate on the question.

Asking for reasonable clarification does not negatively affect scores. However, repeated requests to re-explain basic concepts may be interpreted as unfamiliarity with the subject matter during scoring.

## Off-topic handling

If a candidate's answer drifts significantly off-topic, the AI gently redirects them: _"That's helpful context — to bring us back, I'd like to focus on [original question topic]."_ This keeps sessions on track without penalizing candidates for providing relevant background.

Answers that are completely off-topic or unrelated to the question will score low on the Relevance dimension, so candidates should address the question directly before adding context.

## Tips for candidates

- **Speak your reasoning out loud.** The AI cannot see diagrams or code — explain your thought process as if talking to a colleague who cannot see your screen.
- **Structure longer answers.** Start with a direct answer, then provide supporting detail. This improves Communication Clarity scores.
- **Don't rush past follow-ups.** If the AI asks a follow-up, treat it as a chance to demonstrate deeper knowledge, not as a sign you answered incorrectly.
- **Use technical vocabulary naturally.** Precise terminology is recognized and scored positively on Technical Accuracy, but using jargon without explanation can hurt Communication Clarity.
